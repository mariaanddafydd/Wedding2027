On the Day

A few things worth knowing, in the order they'll happen.

The Mass
12:30–13:30 · Las Calatravas

Our ceremony is a Catholic mass, held in a mix of Spanish and English.

  • Seating: the three front rows are reserved for family, with our witnesses at the front on either side. Facing the altar, the groom's side is on the right and the bride's on the left.
  • What to wear: this is a religious ceremony, so please dress with appropriate coverage. Women are welcome to keep headpieces on in church; men, please take off any hats.
  • Standing and kneeling: at some points during the mass, people kneel. If you'd rather not, it's absolutely fine to stand instead, but please don't stay seated unless it's for medical reasons.
  • Sign of peace: at one point everyone offers a sign of peace to the people around them. In Spain this can be a handshake or a kiss on the cheek, so don't be alarmed if your neighbour leans in!
  • Communion: if you're not Catholic, please don't take communion. You're welcome to stay in your seat while others go up.
  • Leaving the church: Make sure to stay around for our exit. You'll be given flower petals to throw at us as we leave the church!
Cocktail Hour
Approx. 13:45–14:45 · Real Casino de Madrid

After the mass, it's a short walk (2 min) up Calle de Alcalá to the Real Casino de Madrid.

  • On arrival: you'll be asked your name at the door and given your table number. The seating plan will be displayed by the banquet hall.
  • Cloakroom: there'll be a cloakroom at the entrance for coats, a change of shoes and headpieces.
  • Men's dress rule: the Casino doesn't allow men in without a jacket, tie and dress shoes.
  • Jackets stay on: unless it's for health reasons, please keep jackets and ties on until the party starts after the banquet, both to respect the venue's rules and out of tradition.
  • A historic venue: the Casino is one of the most historically significant buildings in Madrid, full of precious artworks and paintings. Please take care around them, especially as the night (and the wine) goes on, so we can avoid any accidents.
The Banquet
Approx. 15:00–18:00 · Lunch

After the cocktail we'll all sit down for a long Spanish lunch, which we expect to finish around 18:00.

  • Our entrance: once everyone is seated, we'll make our grand entrance as a married couple. This will be high energy, with loads of napkin spins! Be prepared to jump up and down with us! Here's a taste of what to expect!
  • Bouquets: just before dessert, we'll hand out bouquets! This is a Spanish tradition. The bouquets go to people we know (or suspect!) are about to get married, or who've helped us in a significant way with organising the wedding. Please don't be offended if you don't receive one. We might just be signaling we know something you don't!
  • Speeches: there won't be any invited speeches. But don't worry, if you think you should have gotten to shame the groom and bride, at our wedding you're welcome to heckle (moderately!) during the banquet. Someone needs to scream the heckle at the start (don't be shy, be that someone!), and then everyone answers. Here's how it goes in each country:

The First Dance
Starts the party 18:00 · watch for your cue!

After the banquet we'll all move to the disco, where the party starts with a slow dance. Here's how it will go:

  1. María opens the dance with her father, and Dafydd joins with his mother.
  2. They swap partners, and María's mother and Dafydd's father join in.
  3. Someone will join as the fourth couple, and that's your cue!

As soon as you see the fourth couple step onto the floor, we'd love every couple to join in, so we can all share a short, big slow dance before the party begins. No experience needed! The sooner the first few couples join, the more everyone else will follow, and the more we are, the less anyone will notice no-one knows how to slow dance!

The Party
Approx. 18:00 to 22:00-23:00 · Open bar & dancing
  • Open bar! There will be a selection of spirits, which you can enjoy with a choice of mixers (think gin & tonic, rum & coke).
  • Smoking: we can only smoke outside. Due to the location of the venue there is no "smoking space" so we encourage people to make those trips short and sweet. The disco is quite large and will have sofas for relaxing in between bops.
The After-Party
22:00-23:00 to 06:00 · nearby disco
  • The plan: when the party at the Casino is finished, it will carry on at a nearby disco. Entries (which will include one last drink at the venue) will be given out to the brave souls who are ready to go party the night away spanish style! Let's see if you can reach 6 am!
  • Late-night snack: If you're hungry after the disco, Chocolatería San Ginés, close to the Casino and the club, serves hot churros and chocolate 24/7. It's the ultimate Madrid post-party destination!

FAQsPreguntas Frecuentes

What time should I arrive at the church?

The ceremony starts promptly at 12:30. We strongly recommend arriving 30 minutes beforehand, so that everyone can find their seat without rushing. The church doors will open at 12:00 and will close at the start of the ceremony to avoid interruptions.

What is the dress code?

Formal. Please see full details below.

Can I bring a guest?

We're only able to accommodate guests named on your invitation, so please check your invitation for any plus-one.

Will the reception be indoors?

Yes, the reception at the Real Casino de Madrid will be entirely indoors, and a cloakroom will be available for coats and bags.

¿La recepción será en interior?

Sí, la recepción en el Real Casino de Madrid será completamente en interior, y habrá guardarropa disponible para abrigos y bolsos.

Is transportation provided?

We won't be providing transport, but both venues are very central and easy to reach by public transport. The Sevilla metro stop (Line 2) sits right alongside the church, and the Real Casino de Madrid is just a short walk from there. Ubers and taxis are readily available across the city.

¿Habrá transporte?

No habrá transporte organizado, pero ambos lugares son muy céntricos y de fácil acceso en transporte público. La parada de Sevilla (Línea 2) está justo al lado de la iglesia, y el Real Casino de Madrid está a 5 metros.

¿Y después de las 22?

La fiesta seguirá en una disco cercana!

Travel & HotelsViaje y Alojamiento

We won't be arranging transport or accommodation, but everything is centrally located and easy to reach by metro or bus. The Sevilla stop (Line 2) sits right alongside the church. If you're travelling from abroad, Madrid's airport is Adolfo Suárez Madrid–Barajas (MAD).

La celebración es en pleno centro de Madrid. Se puede llegar en taxi o en metro, la parada es metro Sevilla (Línea 2), justo al lado de la iglesia. Próximamente añadiremos descuentos con la cadena NH Hoteles.

Good to know: our wedding weekend falls on a long holiday weekend in Spain: Monday 6th December (Día de la Constitución) and Wednesday 8th December (La Inmaculada) are both national public holidays. We'd really recommend booking your flights and accommodation well in advance, as prices may spike over this popular travel period.

Aviso: Nuestra boda coincide con el puente de diciembre, es importante reservar el alojamiento con bastante antelación.

There are plenty of hotels and Airbnbs throughout the city to suit every budget and location. We'll soon be adding discounts with the NH Hotels chain.

Dress CodeCódigo de Vestimenta

Spain has quite strict wedding etiquette regarding attire, so please do your best to follow it, as it's a cultural practice we'd love for you to be part of. This is a religious ceremony, so please dress accordingly.

Ladies: a floor-length or midi dress with appropriate coverage for the church, paired with formal dress shoes. Long sleeves are recommended.
Men: a suit or kilt, tie (not bowties), and dark dress shoes are mandatory.

Headpieces: as it's a daytime wedding, we'd love to see lots of lovely headpieces! We highly recommend Tim de Kir (Calle de Ayala, 59). It's an underground with good prices, and you can pop in the day before and pick one on the spot to match your outfit. We also recommend Pop Up MadrIT (Calle de Goya, 53). Headpieces can be worn in church and through the whole party.

Weather: Madrid in December is chilly, with daytime temperatures around 0–12°C. Most of the day will be spent indoors. Men must bring a jacket as part of their suit or kilt attire. No casual coats.

España tiene una etiqueta de boda bastante estricta respecto a la vestimenta, así que os pedimos que la sigáis lo mejor posible, ya que es una práctica cultural de la que nos encantaría que formarais parte. Al tratarse de una ceremonia religiosa, os pedimos vestir acorde a ella.

Señoras: vestido largo y con cobertura adecuada para la iglesia.
Caballeros: traje, corbata y zapatos de vestir son obligatorios.

Clima: Madrid en diciembre es frío, con temperaturas diurnas de entre 0 y 12°C. La mayor parte del día será en interior, pero recomendamos traer un abrigo.
